Young men are carrying too much in silence.

Monna Madoda is a space where young men can be honest about what's heavy — without judgment, without it leaving the room. We meet in small groups, we talk plainly, and we look out for each other.

Through peer circles, mentorship and practical workshops, we build the things that don't get taught at home or at school: how to name what you feel, how to ask for help, how to handle money, how to be a man your future family would be proud of.

What we work on

Five things that feed each other. We start with honest conversation and move to action.

Gender-based violence

Finding the root causes and having the conversations that prevent harm.

Mental health

Removing the stigma around vulnerability and addressing high suicide rates.

Economic pressure

Unemployment and the expectations men are raised to carry.

Substance dependency

Understanding drinking and drug use as a way of coping, and finding better ways.

Mentorship gap

Connecting young men with older men who have walked the road.

How a circle works

You don't need to prepare anything. You don't need to have it figured out. You just need to show up.

You reach out

Send us a message using the form below. Tell us as much or as little as you want.

We invite you in

We'll share the date, time and place of the next circle near you. If it's your first time, someone will meet you at the door.

You sit with us

Small group, closed doors, no phones. Nobody is forced to speak. What's shared in the circle stays in the circle.

What we ask of each other

Our circles are small, private groups where young men talk openly about the pressures they face and support one another. Nothing here is a performance.

What is shared in the circle stays in the circle. Nobody is pushed to speak before they are ready, and every experience is respected.

Need help right now? Call the SADAG suicide crisis line on 0800 567 567 (South Africa, toll-free, 24 hours).

Questions young men ask us

Do I have to talk?

No. You can come and just listen. There's no pressure to share anything until you're ready. Some men come to three or four circles before they say a word.

Is what I say really private?

Yes. What is said in the circle stays in the circle. We don't record sessions, we don't report back to families or schools, and we don't repeat names. The only exception is if someone is in immediate danger.

Who else will be there?

Young men from around Mahikeng — mostly between 15 and 30. Some are at school, some are working, some are looking for work. Everyone comes for their own reason.

What does it cost?

Nothing. Circles are free. If you want to contribute to keeping them running you can, but it's never expected.

I'm not in Mahikeng. Can I still join?

Reach out anyway. We're growing, and we'd rather hear from you than not. Send us a message and we'll see what's possible.

I'm a parent or teacher — can I refer someone?

Yes. Use the form below and mention who you're referring and how to reach them. We'll follow up gently and privately.
